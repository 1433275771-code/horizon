## [OpenAI&\#x27;s Astra Solves 10 Long-Standing Math Problems](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/1/ten-advances-in-mathematics/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 9.0/10

OpenAI revealed on August 1, 2026 that an internal version of its next model family, Astra, solved ten open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science, spending less than $2,000 per problem on GPT-5.6 Sol token pricing. The results include Lean 4 formalizations in the openai/ten-proofs repository and a paper describing the solutions. This could mark a major step forward in AI reasoning, demonstrating that large language models can produce genuine mathematical discoveries. It may accelerate the shift toward what Terence Tao calls &\#x27;big mathematics,&\#x27; where humans and AI collaborate with machines handling much of the technical groundwork. The solved problems span high-dimensional sphere packing, non-Sophic groups, the Connes rigidity conjecture, arithmetic circuit lower bounds, quantum parallel repetition, the nearest vector problem, and multicolor Ramsey numbers. OpenAI has not disclosed how many problems were attempted without success, and the company also released an LLM-generated PDF where the model reconstructs how the proofs came together based on unpublished reasoning traces.

**Background**: Formal proof verification systems like Lean 4 allow computers to check the correctness of mathematical proofs line by line, providing a strong guarantee beyond traditional peer review. OpenAI says humans organized the model&\#x27;s arguments and formalized them in Lean, while the mathematical reasoning itself was generated by AI. This continues a recent trend of frontier AI labs showcasing research capabilities, following Anthropic&\#x27;s claim that Claude, using Mythos Preview, discovered cryptographic weaknesses for about $100,000 in tokens. The cultural resonance is similar to Deep Blue&\#x27;s 1997 chess victory, prompting existential questions in the mathematical community.

## [NetBSD 11.0 Released with MicroVM Kernel and Updated NPF Firewall](https://blog.netbsd.org/tnf/entry/netbsd_11_0_released) ⭐️ 8.0/10

NetBSD 11.0 was officially released, introducing a new MICROVM kernel for x86 that can boot in about 10 milliseconds and significantly improved NPF firewall filtering. The release also includes numerous hardware improvements and other enhancements. This major version release brings meaningful performance and security advancements to NetBSD, a widely used BSD operating system. The extremely fast-booting MICROVM kernel could enable new use cases in microservices and virtualized environments, while the NPF updates strengthen firewall capabilities for both simple and complex deployments. The MICROVM kernel leverages PVH boot, VirtIO MMIO, and multiple kernel optimizations to achieve its fast boot time, supporting both i386 and amd64 architectures. The NPF firewall improvements include layer 2 filtering and user/group filtering, adding more flexible policy options beyond traditional layer 3/4 rules.

hackernews · jaypatelani · Aug 1, 17:56 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49136736)

**Background**: NetBSD is a free, open-source Unix-like operating system known for its portability, clean design, and support for a wide range of hardware platforms. NPF is NetBSD&\#x27;s stateful packet filter firewall, comparable to Linux&\#x27;s iptables or OpenBSD&\#x27;s PF. The new MICROVM kernel is a specialized kernel configuration tuned for virtual machine environments, aiming to minimize boot latency for service-oriented workloads.

## [Study Probes Symmetry Learning Inside KataGo&\#x27;s Neural Networks](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vcrki2/how_symmetric_are_the_insides_of_a_go_network_r/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

David Wu, the author of the open-source Go program KataGo, published a study analyzing how superhuman Go neural networks handle the board&\#x27;s 8-fold rotational and reflectional symmetries through data augmentation alone. The study reports that the networks partially learn orientation-invariant internal concepts but also memorize per-orientation features, with one unexpected finding. This is significant because it sheds light on whether neural networks exploit known symmetries naturally or compensate by memorization, which informs data augmentation strategies and architecture design for game-playing agents and other symmetric domains. It also contributes to interpretability research from a leading open-source Go AI developer. The models do not enforce symmetry in their architecture; instead, they rely on stochastic 8-fold data augmentation that randomly rotates or reflects each training batch. The write-up was largely AI-generated with detailed human direction and feedback, and the code is linked from the accompanying study page.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/icosaplex · Aug 1, 16:18

**Background**: Go is a board game with complete rotational and reflectional symmetry, so any board position can be transformed into eight equivalent orientations. KataGo is an open-source computer Go program by David Wu that uses deep learning and self-play reinforcement learning, reaching superhuman strength. Data augmentation is a technique that generates modified training samples to improve generalization; in this case, stochastic 8-fold augmentation exposes the network to all orientations during training without explicitly constraining its internal representations. The study asks whether the resulting network builds orientation-invariant concepts or duplicates them per orientation.
